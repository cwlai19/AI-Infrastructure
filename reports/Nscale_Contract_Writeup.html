Key points

  • Neither customer pays before delivery. Anthropic pays nothing until it accepts a tranche; Microsoft pays upfront instalments but gets them back if it cancels a late tranche, and monthly fees start only after acceptance.
  • Lateness has a price, then an exit. Microsoft: a day's bill credited per day late, and cancellation of a tranche 60 days late. Anthropic: no fees during delay, then discounts, then cancellation after a redacted period.
  • Once delivered, revenue is sticky. Both run for years from acceptance (Microsoft 6 years) and can be ended only for uncured breach, insolvency or specified changes of control.
  • Anthropic's deal depends on Nscale's financing. If GPU and data-centre financing is not in place by a redacted longstop, either side can walk; until then Nscale cannot sell the capacity elsewhere.
  • Supplier risk sits with Nscale. NVIDIA or component delays are excused only if Nscale proved binding orders early and gave notice; Microsoft excludes hardware-supplier delays from force majeure.
  • Built for lenders. Microsoft lets Nscale pledge the receivables and requires lender step-in arrangements; Anthropic will negotiate direct agreements with lenders, on terms it must agree.
  • Liability is capped. Microsoft caps each side at one fifth of the SOW's fees; Anthropic's cap is redacted, with payment obligations outside it.
  • Most numbers are redacted (prices, GPU counts, delivery dates, thresholds). The Microsoft SOW's own example dates Tranche 2 delivery to 28 Sep 2027.
